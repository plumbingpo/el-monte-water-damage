Of all the problems an owner can have, sewage coming up through the drains ranks among the most dangerous. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. When it happens in El Monte, El Monte Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs
  • Sewage residue visible around drain openings or on basement floors
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used
  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Route sump pump and gutter water away from the sewer line, which can back up when storm flow overloads it
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • Hair and other debris are easy to stop with simple strainers on tub, shower and sink drains
  • A drain that is slowing down should be dealt with early, while it is still partly open
  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems

Why Hire a Professional for Sewage Cleanup?

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.
