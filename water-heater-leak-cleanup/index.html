Water Heater Leak Cleanup in El Monte: What to Know

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. How bad it gets depends on placement, whether that is the garage, a closet in the hall or an attic sitting above finished rooms, which is the worst.

In El Monte, we start with water extraction and then inspect everything around the heater. The platform beneath the tank and the base of the closet walls are usually soaked. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. Each of those areas is metered, then dried using dehumidifiers and air movers.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Cut the Power or GasElectric units get shut down at the breaker. For gas, turn the control knob to off. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorAnything absorbent, including boxes and rugs, should come up from the wet area. Fabric and cardboard absorb water fast, then keep it pressed to the concrete and walls.
Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Excess pressure in the tank from a failed thermostat or a missing expansion tank.
  • Anode rods that are used up, leaving the steel inside the tank open to rust.
  • Fittings left slightly loose by recent repair or installation work.
  • Years of sediment buildup and corrosion wearing a hole in the base of an old steel tank.
  • A drain valve that loosens, cracks or no longer closes all the way.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Platform and Subfloor

The wooden stand or subfloor under the tank takes the most water. Rot can weaken it, a real concern once a full new tank sits on top.

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

Carpet, hardwood or laminate in the hallway gets wet as water escapes below the closet door. Hot water speeds up swelling in wood flooring and loosens carpet backing.

Stored Belongings

The first casualties are usually the furniture, boxes and keepsakes stored around the heater in a garage or closet. Wet contents may grow mold unless they are dried or thrown out soon, and cardboard simply collapses.

Garage Wall Base

Water crossing a garage slab soaks the bottom plate and drywall of the wall shared with the house, then moves into the flooring on the living side.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

Extract the Water

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

A sudden tank failure is often treated as accidental and sudden, so the resulting damage to belongings, floors and walls is often covered. Replacing the heater is usually not included, because a tank wearing out from age counts as maintenance. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Read your policy, take photos of the damage and the heater, and do not dispose of the old unit before the claim is settled.
