Hardwood Floor Water Damage in El Monte

It does not take long for water to change a wood floor. Boards take on moisture from below and from the edges, swell against each other and start to cup, with the edges rising higher than the center. That does not mean the floor is lost. When drying starts within the first few days, solid hardwood frequently lies flat again. The key is pulling moisture out of the boards and the subfloor underneath at a steady, controlled pace.

We dry hardwood with floor drying mats. The mats are sealed to the surface and connected to a vacuum unit that draws moisture up through the boards and seams, while dehumidifiers keep the room air dry. Compared with carpet or drywall it is slower, commonly a week or more. Hold off on the sander until every board is fully dry. Sand a cupped floor too soon and it will crown later.

What It Looks Like

  • Blackish or gray discoloration at board joints and fasteners
  • Boards lifting off the subfloor or tenting upward at the seams
  • A hazy, whitened or flaking finish where the water sat
  • A springy or soft spot in the floor that was not there earlier
  • Board edges sitting higher than the centers, called cupping
  • New squeaks or popping sounds when you walk across the wet area
Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Rusting Fasteners

Lingering moisture rusts the fasteners, which then hold the boards less firmly. The rust also bleeds into the wood as dark stains that sanding may not remove.

Sanding Too Early

Sanding cupped boards flat while they are still wet removes the raised edges. When the wood finishes drying, the centers end up high and the floor is crowned.

Drying Too Fast

When the top of the wood dries much quicker than the inside, stress builds in the board. Boards may then split, crack or shrink apart for good.

Moisture Under the Boards

Dry feeling boards can sit on a subfloor that remains damp for a long time. Unless that layer is dried as well, expect renewed cupping or mold on the underside.

Our Process

Our Hardwood Floor Water Damage Process

1

Take Baseline Readings

Readings are taken in the boards and the subfloor, and on dry flooring close by as a reference. The comparison shows how far down moisture reached and how likely the floor is to recover.

2

Remove Surface Water

Standing water is extracted right away and rugs and furniture are moved off the wet area. The faster water stops feeding the seams, the less the boards swell.

3

Set Drying Mats

We tape drying mats to the floor and attach them to a vacuum system, which draws moisture upward through the wood. To avoid cracking, dehumidifiers keep conditions steady and the wood dries at an even rate.

4

Monitor and Advise

Readings are logged every one to two days and the equipment stays until the wet zone equals the dry comparison areas. Then we tell you whether the floor needs refinishing, a few boards replaced, or nothing.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Water Goes Under

Most of the water ends up beneath the boards, having run down through the joints. The surface may wipe dry while the underside stays soaked.

24 to 48 hours

Cupping Starts

Swelling sets in and board edges lift. Finish may turn cloudy. Mold can begin on the subfloor and the unfinished underside of the wood.

Day 3 to 7

Boards Start Lifting

Pressure grows through the whole floor as the wood expands. Edges get crushed together, nails work loose and parts of the floor may start rising from the subfloor.

After a week

Beyond Drying

Buckling, black staining and mold under the boards become likely. Drying is usually no longer enough, and the bulk of the wet flooring has to be replaced.
